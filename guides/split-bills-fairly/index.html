50/50 or by income? How to split shared bills fairly

Two common ways to split, worked through with the same four spends, so you can see exactly how the answer changes — and a free calculator that does both.

Step 1 — decide what counts as shared

Before any split, sort spending into shared (the food shop, broadband, a train ticket for both of you) and personal (a cinema ticket only one of you used). Personal spending never goes into the split. Agree this step first and the rest is simple arithmetic.

Step 2 — pick the split

Option A: straight 50/50

Each of you owes half of the shared total. Whoever paid more than half is owed the difference.

Option B: in proportion to what you each earn

Each person's share of the shared costs matches their share of your combined income. If one of you takes home 60% of the household income, they cover 60% of the shared costs.

Which income figure you use (take-home or before tax) is your choice — the maths works either way, as long as you both use the same one. You may also want to weigh things money doesn't show, such as who does more of the childcare or housework; no calculator can decide that for you.

Shared total: £122.00. Partner A paid £68.40 of it; Partner B paid £53.60.

50/50

Each owes £61.00. Partner A paid £7.40 more than half, so Partner B owes Partner A £7.40.

By income (A takes home £2,100, B £1,400)

A earns 60% of the £3,500, so A's share is 60% of £122.00 = £73.20; B's is £48.80. A only paid £68.40 — £4.80 short — so Partner A owes Partner B £4.80.

Same spends, same people — the settle-up flips direction. Neither answer is “right”; they answer different questions. What matters is that you agree which one you're using before the month starts.
