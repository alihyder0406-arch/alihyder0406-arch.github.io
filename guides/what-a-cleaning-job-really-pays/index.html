Guide · cleaners, trades & sole traders

What did that job really pay you per hour?

The price on the quote isn't what you earned. Here's the sum, three real-looking jobs from one small cleaning business, and how to work out the price that would have hit your target.

The sum

Earned per hour = (price charged − materials you actually spent) ÷ hours it actually took.

Two things quietly pull the real rate down: materials come out of the price, and overruns spread the same money over more hours. If you want travel time counted, add it to the hours — the rate falls again, which is exactly why it's worth knowing.

Three jobs, one business

These are the fictional worked-example jobs that ship inside The Quote Builder — the figures are its own results, so you can check them.

What it shows

The kitchen deep clean looks like the best job on price (£118.80) but, after £18 of materials and two hours over, it paid £20.16 an hour. The weekly clean, at a lower price, paid £32.32 — because it took exactly the time quoted.

The price that would have hit your target

Turn the sum round: price needed = target hourly rate × real hours + materials. With a £32 target, the office clean that really takes 4.5 hours and £7 of materials needs to be priced at £151.00, not £72.

This is arithmetic, not pricing advice: your rate, your market and your costs are yours to decide. It doesn't include tax, VAT or National Insurance.
