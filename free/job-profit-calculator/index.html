What did that job really pay you per hour?

The price you quoted isn't what you earned. Take out the materials, divide by the hours it really took, and you get the number that matters.

Enter the price and the hours it actually took.

The sum

Earned per hour = (price − materials) ÷ real hours

In the example, a weekly office clean was quoted at £72 for 4 hours. It took 4.5 hours and £7 of materials, so it paid £14.44 an hour — against a £32 target. At the quoted 4 hours it would have been £16.25. To hit £32 an hour at 4.5 hours, the job needed to be priced at £151.

This is arithmetic, not advice: it doesn't include travel time, tax, VAT or National Insurance unless you put them in the numbers yourself.

One job is a clue. Twenty jobs are a pattern.
